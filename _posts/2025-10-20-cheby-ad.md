This studio enables them to have the knowledge of construction-aware generative modelling control analysis and optimization. The work is focused on the generalizaiton of the of the building system by understanding the geometric dependencies and its constructive constraints which have to be defined and evaluated. The challenge is then to propose enhancements to the system and a study of constructive trade-offs when the building system is applied to an arbitrary geometry. This consultancy oriented approach comes along with the study of different form-finding modelling strategies for efficient structures that are the basis of the next semester. The scope of this part covers curvature and topology, complex systems modelling, dynamic relaxation and optimization. Several intensive and transversal workshops will be deployed to ground these subjects.

Chebyshev nets are a spetial kind of 3D grid composed only of segments of equal length. They are of special interest when designing elastically-bent structures such as gridshells, since the equal length property of these nets guarantees that the grid could be assembled ‘flat’ on-site, and later bent to it’s final shape by either manual or mechanical means.

The Japanese EXPO Pavilion in Hanover 2000 by the famous Architect Shigeru Ban was selected as the case study project as our team topic. The shell surface is made of symmetrical curves on the boundary and sweeped with series of circles, in this way the geometry has a rhythm to the space. Normally the Chebyshev nets were generated with compass method or draping method. Nevertheless, controlling the boundaries was not an ease as these methods will cause incomplete quad cells since we have no clue of how the suitable length would be and where should be the probable starting point. This chebyshev nets were generated by utilizing Kangaroo's diagonal mesh to convert the UV direction of the surface, then starting with an average length as initial value to equalize the net-grid. In such manner we can take advantage of ease controlling the UV division combined with other algorithms on purpose. Here we evaluated the metrics with surface deviation, maximum displacement, and the aspect ratio of the cells.

----

The second phase of the studio is to make an adaptation of the researched structure system to an extraordinarily complicated surface. A constraint of applying the chebyshev nets is associated to the global curvature of the surface. As long as the global curvature is smaller than 2π, the grid-net can cover through the whole surface, otherwise singularity points need to be applied into the grid-net. In this case the difficulty is to create a optimum coarse mesh. The scientist in the computational graphic aspect has some methods utilizing vector fields in CG industry, yet not so many ways to implement them into digital fabrications. Once more we selected some variations to indicate different approaches. One candidate is the default Rhino quad remesh tool. Another one combined with some drainage guidelines. The rest meshes come from InstantMesh, with and without decreasing the counts of singularities.
The meshes generated from the vector field show better results in length deviation and aspect ratio. A further research would be extended if the singularities could be controlled considering assembly and structure behaviour. Consequently we generated an algorithm to clusterize the surface network into pieces, so that they can be arrayed onto the ground for simple assembly.
